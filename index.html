Chemical structures as text, for terminals: coloured Braille, plain ASCII, or a picture through the Kitty graphics protocol. Everything here runs in your browser: RDKit, compiled to WebAssembly, reads the SMILES and lays out and draws the molecule, and molterm's renderer turns the drawing into characters. Nothing is sent anywhere.

How it works

  1. RDKit.js reads the SMILES (or molblock) and computes a 2D layout, as the molterm server does with RDKit in Python.
  2. RDKit draws the bonds on a canvas with no labels, each element in a pure colour, at four pixels per dot.
  3. Each two-by-four block of dots becomes one Braille character; the element whose ink covers most of a cell colours it, with the terminal's own colours, so the drawing follows its theme.
  4. Atom labels are set as real letters, with hydrogens, charges and isotopes, on the side away from the bonds.

The terminal is restty, which shows Kitty graphics in the browser. RDKit (BSD licence). Fonts: DejaVu Sans Mono.
